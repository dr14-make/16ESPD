Step 08 · 15 min

Tire friction against slip

Grip peaks at 4 % slip and falls once the tire slides.

page: step 08 · reference dyad/HandsOn/TireFrictionCurve.dyad

Say: a tire does not grip by rolling perfectly. It grips by slipping a little — the tread deforms — and up to a point, more slip means more force. Past that point it slides, and grip drops.

Timing: 15 minutes. The relations are long; most students should paste them from the page and spend the time on the plot.

κ in, μ out

  • Rises to μA = 1.0 at κ = 0.04 (adhesion).
  • Falls to μS = 0.7 beyond κ = 0.12 (sliding).
  • Each segment is a cubic with zero slope at its ends — no corners for the solver.
  • Point-symmetric: braking slip gives negative friction.

Say: the sweep bench ramps κ from −1 to +1 over 2 s. Plotting against time would show the same curve with a shifted axis; the tuple (x, y) in idxs plots one signal against the other.

Point at: the peak, then the right of it. Past the peak more slip gives less force. Hold that thought: it is why wheelspin runs away in step 10.
