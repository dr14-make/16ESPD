Subject goals

The course gives you a working knowledge of:

  • the basics of control engineering and control theory;
  • the dynamic behavior of a vehicle — and of the driver as a controller;
  • the car's communication bus;
  • electric cars and their components: DC machines, asynchronous and synchronous motors — principles, construction, basic properties and operation;
  • hybrid drive systems;
  • the car's control, safety, communication and comfort electronics.

Say: a car today is a set of systems that talk to each other — engine, brakes, steering, batteries, comfort — and most of them are controlled by software. This course goes through those systems one at a time: what each does, the physics behind it, and how it is controlled.

Point at: the driver as a controller. The driver is part of the loop too: they watch the speed and press the pedal. Many systems in the course either help the driver (cruise control, ABS) or replace them.

How a lecture and a practical work

A typical lecture

  1. Introduction — purpose and functions of the system.
  2. The physics behind it.
  3. Its main components.
  4. Its algorithms and control strategy.

A typical practical

  1. A short reminder of the theory.
  2. Building the model — guided, step by step.
  3. "Playing" with the model to reach the task's goal.
  4. A short report at home, about half a page to one page.

Say: lectures go from "what is it for" down to "how is it controlled". Practicals turn that into a model you build and experiment with. The report is short on purpose: what you tried, what you saw, what it means.
